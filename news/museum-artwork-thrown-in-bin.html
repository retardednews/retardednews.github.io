Museum's Beer-Can Artwork Thrown in the Bin by a Worker Who Thought It Was Litter

The cans were hand-painted masterpieces. The new lift engineer saw two empty beers and did the tidy thing.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 8, 2024 · Lisse, Netherlands · 2 min read

Modern art is often accused of looking like rubbish. At one Dutch museum, someone took that literally.

The LAM museum in Lisse was displaying a work by French artist Alexandre Lavet called All The Good Times We Spent Together. It appears to be two empty, dented beer cans. In fact, they are meticulously hand-painted with acrylics, a piece the museum says took enormous time and effort.

To surprise visitors, the museum placed the cans in its glass lift shaft, as though someone had carelessly left them behind. The illusion worked a little too well.

Doing his job

A lift engineer who had just started working at the museum spotted the cans, decided they were trash, and threw them away.

A curator returning from a short break noticed the artwork was gone and found it in a bin bag full of swept-up litter, just before it was taken away for good. Both cans survived intact.

A museum spokesperson said there were no hard feelings, and that the engineer was only doing his job.

The cans were cleaned up and moved to a traditional plinth so the artwork could rest after its adventure. The museum's director said the whole point of the collection is to make people look at everyday objects in a new way. Mission, arguably, accomplished.
