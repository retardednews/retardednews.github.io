Family Out Harvesting in Kansas Films a Patchy, Two-Toned Bear. In a State With No Resident Bears

A state biologist confirmed it was a piebald black bear, a genetic oddity he had never seen in Kansas before.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 12, 2026 · Near Syracuse, Kansas, USA · 2 min read

Kansas is not bear country. The state's wildlife department says it is not aware of any bears living there year-round.

So a family harvesting their field about 13 miles north of Syracuse, in western Kansas, did a double take when a bear wandered into view. Then they did a triple take, because the bear was patchy.

Instead of the usual solid dark coat, it had blotches of different colours, and the family got it on video.

The expert verdict

Matt Peek, a wildlife research biologist with the Kansas Department of Wildlife and Parks, identified it as a piebald black bear, a very rare genetic anomaly that causes the patchy fur.

Peek said it was the first piebald bear he had seen in Kansas, though bears in that part of the state have a history of cinnamon-coloured fur.

Rare bear, rarer coat, in a state that barely has bears at all.

Almost all confirmed bears in Kansas are yearlings wandering in from neighbouring states after leaving their mothers. This one, it seems, chose to make an entrance.
