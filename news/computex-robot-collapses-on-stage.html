A Humanoid Robot Walks Out to Help Present a Big Tech Keynote. Its Handlers Were Ready for What Happened Next

The audience was watching, and the robot had a gadget to show off. The company later insisted everything worked exactly as designed.

By RetardedNews Staff · July 30, 2026 · Taipei, Taiwan · 3 min read

Computex in Taipei is one of the biggest tech shows in the world, and this year humanoid robots were everywhere. So when a keynote at the 2026 show brought out a walking, two-legged robot running on chip maker Qualcomm's new robotics platform, the idea was to show the future arriving on schedule.

The robot strode out alongside a presenter, carrying a gadget to show the audience. It got a few steps in.

Then it pitched forward and hit the stage face first with a loud thud. It managed to lift one fist, as if trying to push itself up, but it could not get back on its feet.

The cloth

What happened next made the clip go viral. Members of the demo team hurried on stage, draped a black cloth over the fallen machine like a sheet over a magician's cage, and carried it off. The demonstration was over.

The video only reached a wide audience weeks after the show, and once it did, people could not stop watching. One design site admitted the clip got funnier every time it was played.

Qualcomm's explanation was calm. It said a brief communication glitch had caused a fault in the prototype, and that the robot then carried out a built-in safe collapse routine to protect the people and objects around it. In other words, the company said, the safety systems did their job.

Qualcomm said the robot's "safety systems functioned as designed", adding that live demos always carry some risk and the fall would help improve the software.

Not everyone was convinced by the word 'collapse sequence', since the video shows a robot going down like a felled tree rather than kneeling gracefully. Qualcomm also later stressed that the machine was not its own robot, only one using its chips. Reports name it as the 4NE-1 from German maker NEURA Robotics.
