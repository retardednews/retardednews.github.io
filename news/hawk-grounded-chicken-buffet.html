Hawk Found Stranded on a Driveway Has No Injuries at All. Vets Work Out the Real Reason She Can't Fly

X-rays, toxicology and eye checks all came back clean. Then the neighbours started talking about their missing chickens.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 2, 2026 · Roanoke, Virginia, USA · 3 min read

When a woman in Roanoke, Virginia, found a red-shouldered hawk sitting on her driveway and refusing to fly away, she did the right thing and took her to the Southwest Virginia Wildlife Center, about a mile away.

Staff logged the bird as patient RSHA 26-2599 and went through the full checklist. Broken bones? No. Lead poisoning? No. Eye problems, head trauma, bruises from a fight or a crash? No, no and no. She was not starving either. Quite the opposite.

The diagnosis

The vets could not even feel her keel, the breastbone they normally check to judge a bird's condition, because it was buried under a thick layer of fat. She weighed 910 grams, a little over two pounds, when a female of her species should top out at about a pound and a half.

Her official diagnosis, according to the center: "she was too darn fat to fly."

That raised a new question: who had been feeding her? The team wondered if she was an escaped falconry bird or a hawk someone was keeping illegally. So they asked the finder whether anyone nearby kept free-range chickens.

A few weeks later the answer came back. A neighbour's free-roaming flock had been shrinking at an alarming rate. Every morning the chickens were let out, and every morning the hawk had her pick of the buffet. Then she would eat until full and doze in the trees until she was hungry again.

The center laughs about it, but it says the story has a serious side. A hawk that can't fly is easy prey for bigger predators. Its advice is not to feed wildlife, even by accident, and to cover chicken runs.

The patient is now on a strict diet with daily laps of the flight pen. Once she is slim enough she will be released near where she was found. Staff admit there is a small risk she will remember where the chickens live.
