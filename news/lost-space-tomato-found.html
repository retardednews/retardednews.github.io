Astronaut Accused of Secretly Eating a Space Tomato Is Cleared When It Turns Up 8 Months Later

It floated away on the International Space Station. Everyone assumed he ate it. He didn't.

By RetardedNews Staff · December 6, 2023 · International Space Station · 3 min read

It is one of the great mysteries of modern spaceflight. Not dark matter. Not black holes. A missing tomato.

NASA astronaut Frank Rubio harvested a tiny tomato grown aboard the International Space Station, put it in a bag, and took it out to show some students. He thought he had velcroed it back in place afterwards. He had not. It floated off into the station and vanished.

Rubio spent 18 to 20 hours searching for it without success and concluded it had probably dried out beyond recognition. His crewmates, and much of the internet, had a different theory: he ate it. He denied this.

Exonerated in orbit

Rubio went on to spend more than a year in space, a record for an American astronaut, after a problem with his Soyuz spacecraft doubled his planned stay. He returned to Earth with the tomato question still hanging over him.

Then, on December 6, 2023, during a livestream for the station's 25th anniversary, astronaut Jasmin Moghbeli announced that the crew had found it.

Moghbeli told viewers the crew could finally exonerate their former crewmate.

NASA later revealed there were actually two lost tomatoes, found together in a plastic bag, dehydrated and slightly squished but free of visible mould. The station is bigger than a six-bedroom house, which leaves plenty of corners for fruit to hide. The famous tomatoes were thrown away rather than brought home, ending their journey with no honours at all.
