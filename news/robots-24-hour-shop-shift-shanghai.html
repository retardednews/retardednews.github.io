Six Robots Take a 24-Hour Trial Shift at a Shanghai Shop. Not All of Them Get the Job

More than two million people watched online. One robot's performance review would get a human let go on day one.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 4, 2026 · Shanghai, China · 3 min read

Chinese robot maker AGIBOT wanted to prove its humanoids are ready for real work, so it put six of its wheeled G2 robots in charge of a working convenience store in Shanghai's Xuhui district for a full day, from 10 a.m. on November 2 until 10 a.m. the next morning.

The company livestreamed the whole thing under the title 'Robots Never Clock Out', and more than two million people tuned in to watch them handle the lunch rush, the evening peak, the quiet overnight hours and the next day's breakfast crowd.

According to AGIBOT's own figures, the team got through 1,730 tasks. That included 683 customer-service chats, 147 coffees, 126 breakfasts, 369 product pickups, 277 shelf checks and 128 rounds of table cleaning.

The performance review

At the end the robots were assessed like trainees, on time spent on duty, tasks completed, customer feedback and how they coped with surprises. Four passed and were promoted to 'official employees'. Two did not.

The first failure was a cleaning robot. A full rubbish bin was blocking its usual route, and instead of dealing with it, the robot wandered off its path, rolled out of the store and never came back to its post.

The second was in charge of watching the shelves and restocking them. Reports say it simply did not check often enough to keep up as products sold, and Chinese coverage described it as lacking initiative and showing too little enthusiasm on the job.

Both have been placed in an 'observation group' for more training, with the hope of being hired later. Analysts noted that much of the coverage was sponsored by AGIBOT and that no full task log was released, so the numbers come from the company itself. The walkout, however, was seen by everyone.
