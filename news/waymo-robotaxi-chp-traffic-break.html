Driverless Taxi Spots a Gap Beside a Police Roadblock and Tries to Sneak Through. The Cops Have Other Ideas

Human drivers waited in line. The robot decided it had somewhere better to be. A week later it had a much bigger problem.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 6, 2026 · Interstate 280, San Francisco Bay Area, California, USA · 3 min read

On Friday, November 6, the California Highway Patrol was clearing debris from northbound Interstate 280 near John Daly Boulevard in the Bay Area. To protect the crews, officers ran a traffic break, in which patrol cars spread across the lanes and slowly hold everyone behind them.

The human drivers did what humans do. They queued up and waited.

The robot has a plan

Then a Waymo robotaxi arrived. Instead of joining the line, the driverless car began hunting for a gap next to the police vehicles. Video shared online shows it inching towards an opening, apparently weighing up its chances.

It never got there. A CHP cruiser slid into the gap and shut it. The Waymo backed off into the queue and stayed there like everybody else.

"California Highway Patrol Blockade 1, Waymo 0." (Caption from the person who shared the footage, via MotorBiscuit)

CHP confirmed the incident to local media. It said the block was improvised on the spot, and that it hadn't been in contact with Waymo or the car's remote operators either before or afterwards. Nobody was ticketed.

The bigger problem

The roadblock stunt was not Waymo's only bad freeway week. A day earlier, on November 5, three of its driverless cars drove into an active construction closure on US 101 near Burlingame.

On November 11, California's DMV banned Waymo from offering driverless rides on freeways, calling the repeated wandering into construction zones an "imminent hazard to public safety". Waymo then paused driverless freeway service in every city it serves, including San Francisco, Los Angeles, Miami, Phoenix and Austin. The company says safety is its foundation, that nobody was hurt and nothing was hit, and that its cars are still carrying passengers on ordinary streets. They will just be waiting in line with the rest of us.
