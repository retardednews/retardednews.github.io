A €145,000 Golden Sculpture Vanishes From a Busy Art Fair in 15 Minutes. Police Say It Wasn't About the Gold

Now the gallery is asking everyone who walked past for an unusual favour.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 12, 2026 · Cologne, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany · 3 min read

Art Cologne, one of Germany's oldest and biggest art fairs, was winding down on Sunday, November 8. At booth C-121, run by Düsseldorf's Galerie Ludorff, a small gold-coloured sculpture stood on its pedestal.

Somewhere between 4:55 and 5:10 p.m., it stopped standing there. Someone took it, and the pedestal was left bare.

The piece is S'élevant, or Rising, a 1962 gilded bronze by Hans Arp, the Franco-German artist known for smooth, curving, organic shapes. It is only 28 centimetres tall, about the height of a wine bottle, which presumably helped. Reports value it at between €135,000 and €145,000.

Not a scrap-metal job

Cologne police were quick to rule out one motive. The metal itself is worth very little, a spokesperson said, so whoever took it was not planning to melt it down.

That points to the art market, and the gallery is betting the sculpture's distinctive rising curves will give it away if it is ever offered for sale. Dealers and collectors have been asked to keep watch.

It has also made an unusual appeal. Anyone who was near booth C-121 during those 15 minutes and took photos or videos is asked to send them in, in case something in the background shows who walked off with it.

The gallery called the theft a regrettable shadow over an otherwise very successful fair, and says it hopes the sculpture turns up unharmed.
