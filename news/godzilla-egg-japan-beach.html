Godzilla Egg? Giant Mystery Metal Ball Washes Up on Japanese Beach and the Bomb Squad Is Called In

The internet guessed bombs, UFOs and kaiju. An oceanographer took one look and solved it.

By RetardedNews Staff · February 22, 2023 · Hamamatsu, Japan · 2 min read

When a giant metal sphere appears on your beach, you have two options. Ignore it, or call the bomb squad. Japan chose the bomb squad.

In February 2023, a hollow iron ball almost 1.5 metres wide was discovered on a beach in Hamamatsu, south-west of Tokyo. A local jogger told the broadcaster NHK it had been sitting there for about a month, and that it would not move when he pushed it.

Police sealed off the beach and sent in explosives experts. X-rays showed the ball was hollow and not dangerous, but officials did not say what it actually was. The internet was happy to fill the gap.

Godzilla, UFO or spy?

Social media users dubbed it a Godzilla egg. Other theories included a bomb, an alien craft and something from outer space. It did not help that Japan was already on edge over suspected spy balloons.

The city eventually concluded it was most likely a foreign-made buoy. An oceanographer in Scotland agreed, telling the BBC he recognised it immediately as the kind of float used to hold ocean instruments.

He explained that such buoys can drift for decades and lose their markings, and look a lot like old sea mines, minus the spikes.

Within days, a heavy lifting machine hauled the ball away to be stored and later disposed of. One official said they were relieved it was finally gone. Godzilla, as far as we know, has not come looking for it.
