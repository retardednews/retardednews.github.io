A Dutch Woman Wins the World Porridge Championship in Scotland. Her Secret Came in Her Suitcase

The traditional category allows only three ingredients: oats, water and salt. She beat 30 rivals from 14 countries by thinking hard about one of them.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 3, 2026 · Carrbridge, Highlands, Scotland · 3 min read

Every October the Highland village of Carrbridge hosts the World Porridge Making Championship. The winner takes home the Golden Spurtle, named after the wooden stick Scots use to stir their oats.

The main contest is strict. In the traditional category you may use oats, water and salt, and nothing else. No milk, no sugar, no honey. With only three ingredients allowed, small details decide everything.

This year the title went to Gina Saarberg, 28, from Zutphen in the Netherlands. She beat 30 competitors from 14 countries, including Norway and Australia, in front of a judging panel led by former Gleneagles chef Neil Mugg.

The suitcase ingredient

Her oats were Dutch pinhead oats from a company called De Halm. Her salt was ordinary salt. But her water did not come from a Scottish tap. She brought it with her from home.

"It's very soft and I think the mineral content may have something to do with it," she told BBC Scotland, as reported by DutchNews.

Saarberg started making porridge as a student to save money on food. Over time she switched from rolled oats to oatmeal, which she says made it creamier, almost like rice pudding. She only started adding salt for competitions, and says it brings out more flavour.

She qualified by winning the Dutch national championship earlier in the year, using the same recipe, the same oats and, yes, the same water.

The speciality prize, where anything goes, went to Australian Rob Reid for a Sicilian-Scottish porridge cannoli. Scotland's national dish keeps travelling a long way to be beaten in its own village.
