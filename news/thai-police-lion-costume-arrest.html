Cops Disguise Themselves as a Dancing Lion to Pounce on Burglar Who Robbed a Police Chief 3 Times

He always ran when he saw officers. He did not run from a giant papier-mache lion at a temple fair.

By RetardedNews Staff · February 19, 2026 · Nonthaburi, Thailand · 3 min read

Thai police had a problem. Their suspect, a serial burglar, had a simple strategy: every time he spotted an officer, he ran. So the officers stopped looking like officers. They started looking like a lion.

The man was accused of breaking into the Bangkok home of a local police commander not once, not twice, but three times in early February 2026. The haul was worth about 2 million baht, roughly $64,000, and included Buddhist amulets and artifacts, among them two foot-tall Buddha statues.

Investigators followed the trail of stolen amulets he had sold and learned he liked to visit temples in Nonthaburi province, on the edge of Bangkok. After weeks of surveillance, they came up with a plan worthy of a heist movie.

Enter the lion

On February 18, at a Lunar New Year temple fair, plainclothes officers joined a lion dance procession hidden inside a red-and-yellow lion costume. As the 33-year-old suspect wandered through the festivities, the lion slowly danced its way toward him.

Then the officer holding the lion's head lunged, and the lion pinned the burglar to the ground.

Colleagues rushed in to help, and Thailand's Metropolitan Police Bureau proudly released photos and video of what may be the most festive arrest in history.

Police said the man confessed and told them he stole to fund gambling and drugs. He already had prior convictions for theft. He also, presumably, now has a lifelong fear of parades.
