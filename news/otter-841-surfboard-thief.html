Fugitive Otter 841 Keeps Stealing Surfboards and Outsmarting Wildlife Officials

Nets, traps and a decoy surfboard all failed. Santa Cruz's most wanted sea otter just dived off and kept going.

By RetardedNews Staff · July 20, 2023 · Santa Cruz, California, USA · 3 min read

Surfers off Santa Cruz spent the summer of 2023 dealing with an unusual hazard. Not sharks, not rip currents, but a five-year-old sea otter with a surfboard habit.

Known officially as Otter 841, the female southern sea otter developed a routine: swim up to a surfer, climb aboard, refuse to leave, and start biting the board. In one case she chewed off part of a board's nose.

Crime may run in the family. Otter 841 was born in captivity and released into the wild in June 2020, and her mother had also been captured for harassing kayakers.

The great otter manhunt

Teams from the California Department of Fish and Wildlife and the Monterey Bay Aquarium spent weeks trying to catch her. They towed a baited surfboard toward their boat. They tried several kinds of nets. They deployed an underwater trap operated by divers.

Every time they got close, she simply dived off and swam away. Officials could not net her in open water, and sedating her was off the table because she could drown.

A local photographer said the failed capture attempts had become almost comical.

Federal wildlife officials called her behaviour highly unusual and suggested hormones or people feeding her might be to blame. Meanwhile, she gained a fan club that gathered on the cliffs every day to watch. No humans were hurt, and as of late July she was still at large. Southern sea otters, once down to about 50 animals in 1938, now number around 3,000. At least one of them is very good at surfing.
