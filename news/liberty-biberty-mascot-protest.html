Dozens March on an Insurance Giant's Boston Headquarters. Their Enemy Is Small, Yellow and Fluffy

The organiser called it the next Boston Tea Party. Then a lone counter-protester turned up in costume.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 25, 2026 · Boston, Massachusetts, USA · 3 min read

On a Sunday afternoon in late October, several dozen people gathered outside Liberty Mutual's headquarters in Boston carrying protest signs. Some read 'Liberty from Biberty'. Others showed a cartoon face crossed out.

Their target was Liberty Biberty, the round, fuzzy yellow mascot the insurance company has been putting in its TV adverts. A lot of viewers find him unbearable, and the ads painfully repetitive.

The protest was organised by Connor Harvey, a 19-year-old YouTuber from Wilmot, New Hampshire. His video 'Death of Mascots' tore into the character's looks, and his call to march on headquarters pulled in more than 600,000 views.

A revolution, livestreamed

Protesters told reporters the campaign felt lazy, and compared Biberty unfavourably with rival mascots like the Geico Gecko and Jake from State Farm. Harvey livestreamed the event and promised to keep it peaceful.

"This is a revolution, this is the next Boston Tea Party," Harvey declared, although he admitted the whole thing started as a joke and was unlikely to change the company's mind.

The mascot did have one supporter. A lone counter-protester, Calvin Holmes, showed up dressed as Liberty Biberty himself and was jeered by the crowd. He argued that everyone there was discriminating against 'this puffball' just for the way he was born.

Liberty Mutual did not comment on the protest. It has, however, said that early testing showed Liberty Biberty scoring above average for grabbing attention and making people remember the brand. A crowd, a viral video and a costumed defender on the steps of its headquarters suggest the testing may have been right.
