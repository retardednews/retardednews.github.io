Food Delivery App Lets AI Draw Pictures of the Menu. Customers Order a Jacket Potato and Get Something Very Different

It began with a breakfast sandwich from a café in Northern Ireland. By the time anyone noticed, a bowl of tiger prawns had grown fur.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 21, 2026 · Northern Ireland, UK · 3 min read

Plenty of small takeaways never get round to photographing their dishes, so Just Eat, one of the UK's biggest food delivery apps, came up with a shortcut. Where a restaurant had no picture, an AI tool would read the name of the dish and generate one, with a small 'AI-generated' label in the corner.

The idea was to save independent restaurants the cost of a photographer. The problem is that a name on a menu is not always a description, and the AI took every word literally.

A café in Northern Ireland sells a classic local breakfast: bacon and egg on soda bread. The app's version showed a tall glass of fizzy cola with a fried egg, rashers of bacon and a sausage bobbing around in it.

Then people started looking

Once screenshots hit Reddit and Instagram, users went hunting for more, and there were plenty. A jacket potato became a winter puffer jacket printed with potatoes. A 'chip muffin', a takeaway's name for a chip butty, turned into sweet muffins with fries baked inside.

Garlic bread plain, misspelt on one menu as 'plane', came out as an aeroplane made of toasted garlic bread. A cheese roll turned into sushi with slabs of cheddar. A pot of burger sauce had a tiny burger sitting in it, and a dish of tiger prawns arrived as a bowl of actual tiger cubs swimming in sauce.

Just Eat apologised and paused the trial. A spokesperson told The Journal: "We're sorry for any inaccurate images that have been posted."

The company is now pulling the strange pictures from its listings and says restaurants can always upload their own photos. It has not given up on AI images, though. It says it will keep working on the tool, so somewhere out there the tiger cubs may yet return.
