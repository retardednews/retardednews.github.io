Man Lives 120 Days in a Steel Capsule at the Bottom of the Sea, Then Pops Up for Champagne and a Cigar

No shower, four cameras and a copy of Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea by the bed.

By RetardedNews Staff · January 25, 2025 · Off the Caribbean coast of Panama · 3 min read

Most people find a week of working from home isolating. One man spent four months working from the bottom of the ocean.

On January 24, 2025, a 59-year-old German aerospace engineer climbed out of a capsule submerged 11 metres below the surface off Panama's Caribbean coast. He had been living inside it for 120 days.

His underwater home measured about 30 square metres and was connected by a tube with a narrow spiral staircase to a chamber above the waves, where food, visitors and a doctor could reach him. Inside he had a bed, a toilet, a TV, a computer, internet and an exercise bike. What he did not have was a shower.

Watched around the clock

Four cameras recorded his every move, both to keep an eye on his mental health and to prove he never sneaked up to the surface. Witnesses monitored him day and night.

A Guinness adjudicator confirmed the record for the longest time living underwater without decompression, beating the previous mark of 100 days. He kept a copy of Jules Verne's Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea by his bed and says he admires Captain Nemo.

He said the point was to show that the ocean could one day be a place for humans to live.

He celebrated his return to dry land with champagne, a cigar and a jump straight back into the Caribbean. He admitted he was a little sad the adventure was over. Whoever got to sit next to him afterwards, given the shower situation, was not asked for comment.
