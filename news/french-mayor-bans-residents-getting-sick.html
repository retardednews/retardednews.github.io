French Mayor Signs an Official Order: Nobody in Town Is Allowed to Get Sick

Residents who feel something coming on are told to postpone it. The mayor, a former family doctor, admits the whole thing is absurd. That is the point.

By RetardedNews Staff · September 30, 2026 · Marmande, Lot-et-Garonne, France · 3 min read

Marmande is a market town of tomatoes and vineyards in south-western France. As of this autumn, it is also a place where, on paper, falling ill is against the rules.

Mayor Joël Hocquelet, a Socialist who used to work as a GP, has published a formal municipal decree declaring that it is forbidden for anyone to be ill within the town's boundaries.

The decree even comes with advice. Anyone who feels an illness coming on is asked to put it off until they can get an appointment at the local hospital.

Why a doctor would ban being sick

The order has no legal force, and Hocquelet happily calls it absurd. It is a protest, and the target is what is happening to the town's hospital.

The Marmande-Tonneins hospital, which serves about 110,000 people including part of the neighbouring Gironde, was placed under temporary administration by the regional health authority in January because of serious money problems. From early 2027, departments such as cardiology, digestive medicine and diabetes care may be moved to the hospital in Agen, a long drive away.

Hocquelet, who chairs the hospital's supervisory board in turns with the mayor of nearby Tonneins, warns that further cuts could cost the town its full emergency department and its laboratory, leaving the hospital a shell of itself. Newcomers to the area already struggle to find a family doctor.

He says people are welcome to laugh at the decree as long as they also notice the problem behind it. Large parts of rural France are now described as medical deserts, and he wants the ban to sound the alarm in Paris.

He is not the first to try it. In January 2025 the mayor of Belcastro, a small town in southern Italy, issued a very similar ban on illness to protest about a health centre that was often closed. The joke, it seems, travels well.
