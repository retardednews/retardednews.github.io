Man Runs a Mile in Just Over Five Minutes. He Stopped Four Times on the Way

Each stop was a puzzle, and the clock never paused while he solved it. Here is how long each one took.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 6, 2026 · Toronto, Ontario, Canada · 3 min read

Running a mile in about five minutes is fast for most people. George Scholey, a British speedcubing champion, decided to make it harder. Before every lap he had to solve a scrambled Rubik's Cube.

The attempt took place on October 12 on the track at Central Tech high school in Toronto, in a stiff wind. The format was simple and cruel. When the horn sounded he lifted a cover off a scrambled cube, solved it, then sprinted a 400-metre lap. Then another cube. Then another lap.

There was no pause button. Every second spent twisting the cube counted toward his mile time. To make the total an exact mile, the final lap was a little over nine metres longer than the others.

The numbers

His cube solves took about 9, 10.5, 8.5 and 9.5 seconds, according to Guinness World Records. His laps came in at roughly 67 to 70 seconds each. His official time: 5 minutes 9.25 seconds for the fastest puzzle cube mile.

He trained for seven weeks, splitting the work between sprinting and cubing. Before the attempt he told Guinness he had solved a cube in 3.86 seconds and run a mile in 4:50 in a relay. Doing both at once is a different matter.

Afterwards he told Guinness he was "absolutely buzzing."

It is not his first strange record. Scholey has solved 500 cubes on a skateboard, 6,931 cubes in 24 hours, and about 520 cubes while running the London Marathon. That last one has since been beaten by a Spanish runner who solved 561.

The cube mile is now his fourth multitasking speedcubing title, and the only one where he had to sprint.
