Police Are Called to a Sidewalk Standoff in Philadelphia. One Side Is a 78-Year-Old Professor. The Other Is Carrying Someone's Lunch

She calls it a protest. Police call it disorderly conduct. The other party has declined to comment.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 19, 2026 · Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, USA · 3 min read

Just after 1 p.m. on October 14, Philadelphia police were called to the 1200 block of Filbert Street, near Reading Terminal Market, after a report of 'criminal mischief'.

When officers arrived, they found Carolyn Marvin, a 78-year-old retired communications professor from the University of Pennsylvania, standing on the sidewalk and refusing to step aside. What she was refusing to make way for was an autonomous food delivery robot working for Uber Eats.

Officers told her several times to move. She would not, saying she had a First Amendment right to protest on a public sidewalk. She was handed a criminal violation notice for disorderly conduct as a small crowd looked on.

Why she did it

This was not a chance encounter. Marvin had blocked the robots before, as a protest against the growing fleet of machines rolling around Center City since Uber Eats brought them in earlier this year.

She told reporters she objects on three counts: the robots take work from low-paid human delivery drivers, they crowd the sidewalks, and their cameras are constantly filming the public.

"I was under the impression that I had more rights than the robot did," Marvin said. "I wasn't impeding any persons. I was impeding a machine."

She plans to fight the charge in court. Asked by a local TV station whether she will keep standing in front of robots, her answer was short: 'Why not?'
