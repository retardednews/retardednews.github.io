Court Rules a Fart Is Not Free Speech After Man Deliberately Lets One Rip at Police

He was fined €500. He appealed on free-expression grounds. The judge was not moved.

By RetardedNews Staff · April 14, 2021 · Vienna, Austria · 2 min read

Some legal battles define a nation's values. This one was about a fart.

On June 5, 2020, police in a Vienna park stopped a man sitting on a bench with friends for a routine ID check. According to police, he rose slightly from the bench, looked the officers straight in the eye, and released what they described as a massive fart.

Vienna police fined him €500 for offending public decency. They explained on Twitter that nobody gets fined for an accidental toot, but this man had already been provocative and uncooperative earlier that day. They added, drily, that their officers do not much enjoy being farted at.

The appeal

The man fought the fine. In April 2021, Vienna's administrative court reduced it to €100 because of his clean record and finances. But the judge firmly rejected his argument that the fart was a protected form of expression.

Farts and burps, the ruling said, have no communicative content, and even if they did, they would go beyond the bounds of decency.

The court record described in loving detail how he lifted his buttocks before letting go, while his friends laughed and he grinned at the officers.

His lawyer insisted it was either unintentional or free speech, and vowed to take the matter all the way to Austria's Constitutional Court on principle. The rest of us are left to wonder what the founding fathers of Austrian law would have made of it.
