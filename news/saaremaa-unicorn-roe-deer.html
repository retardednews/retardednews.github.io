A Camera Set Up to Watch Minks in an Estonian Forest Films Something Else Entirely. Its Owners Call It a Unicorn

The animal in the clip has one thing sticking straight out of the middle of its forehead. Experts still can't agree on what it is.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 14, 2026 · Saaremaa, Estonia · 2 min read

On Saaremaa, Estonia's biggest island, the Lutreola Foundation keeps trail cameras in the woods for one reason: to keep an eye on minks. The foundation works to bring back the European mink, one of the rarest mammals on the continent.

Trail cameras film whatever walks past, though, and recently one of them recorded a visitor the team had never seen before.

One horn, dead centre

It was a roe deer, a common sight in Estonian forests. What was not common was its head. Instead of a pair of antlers on top, it had a single growth poking out from the middle of its forehead, pointing forward like something from a fairy tale.

The foundation shared the clip on Facebook and did not hold back, announcing that a unicorn had been found on Saaremaa and joking that little girls' dreams had finally come true.

The Estonian weekly Maaleht asked experts what they were looking at. From the footage alone, they couldn't say for sure. It might be a misplaced antler. It might even be an extra ear growing out of the centre of the deer's forehead.

Deer antlers normally sprout from two bony bases on the top of the skull, which is why a lone growth in the middle of the face looks so strange.

The unicorn was not the only surprise on the memory card. The same camera also caught a black stork, a shy forest bird that is far harder to spot than its white cousin.
