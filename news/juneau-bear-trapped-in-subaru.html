Neighbour Hears a Car Horn Blaring at 12:30 a.m. and Looks Outside. Someone Is Sitting on It

The driver couldn't get out, the owners couldn't unlock the doors, and the police had to improvise with a rope.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 26, 2026 · Juneau, Alaska, USA · 3 min read

At about 12:30 one Monday morning in Juneau, Alaska, a car horn started blaring and would not stop. A neighbour looked out to see who was leaning on it.

It was a black bear, sitting on the horn inside Michael Wilson's Subaru. The neighbour called 911.

Locked in

The bear had let itself in through an unlocked door. Then the door swung shut behind it, and it couldn't work out how to open it again. Wilson said it had already been into three cars on the street that night. His was the only one it got stuck in.

Officers woke the family to ask for the keys, but that didn't help. The bear had ripped out so much wiring that the doors would no longer unlock. So Juneau police tied a rope to the door and pulled it open from a safe distance.

Within five to ten minutes the bear was free. It ran off unhurt, leaving behind bent metal, shredded seats, torn door panels, and, according to Mistee Wilson, a little something extra on the floor.

"So I got to hand it to Subaru. They made a good car," Michael Wilson told KTUU. "It couldn't get out."

The praise came with a sting: the family only had liability insurance, so the damage isn't covered. They say bears have been unusually bold this year after a winter of more than 200 inches of snow and a late spring cut into the berries and fish they normally eat.

Wilson's advice to his neighbours is simple: lock your car, and don't leave any food inside. Not even candy.
