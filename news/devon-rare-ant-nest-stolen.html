Someone Takes a Spade to an English Nature Reserve and Makes Off With Up to 5,000 Captives

Researchers found a hollow where something precious used to be. Police are investigating, and a conservation charity compares it to stealing rare bird eggs.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 2, 2026 · Chudleigh Knighton, Devon, England · 3 min read

In mid-October, researchers doing their regular checks at Chudleigh Knighton Heath, a nature reserve in south Devon, found a hollow in the ground where something should have been. The soil around it showed clear marks of a spade.

Someone had dug up and carried away an entire ant nest.

Not just any ants

These were narrow-headed ants, the rarest ant species in England. The reserve, run by Devon Wildlife Trust, is their last stronghold in the country. The latest survey there counted 142 active nests, and now there is one fewer.

A single nest can hold between 1,000 and 5,000 ants, plus one or more breeding queens that can live for up to 25 years. Colonies grow slowly, so a mature nest represents years of work that can't simply be replaced.

"This is a crime every bit as serious as illegal raids on wild bird eggs," said Julia Balfour of Devon Wildlife Trust.

The heath is a protected Site of Special Scientific Interest, and Devon and Cornwall Police say it is a criminal offence to deliberately or recklessly damage or disturb it. The theft has been reported to the force, and the trust is asking anyone who knows anything to come forward.

Nobody knows why anyone would want them. The species has been disappearing for a century as heathland was built on and farmed, and the trust and the charity Buglife had only this summer started a new project to protect it. Outside Devon, a few colonies survive in Scotland, including on the royal Balmoral estate.
