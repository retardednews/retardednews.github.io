His Campaign Signs Said 'I Barely Want to Do This.' Then the Votes Came In

His website admitted he had no idea what he was doing. A councillor with 15 years at city hall was about to find out how much voters cared.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 19, 2026 · Kelowna, British Columbia, Canada · 3 min read

Most people running for office try to sound eager. Brady Rogers, a 33-year-old small business owner in Kelowna, British Columbia, went the other way.

His lawn signs read "I barely want to do this", "Just trying to pay my rent", "Fine. I'll do it" and "How hard can it be". His campaign website asked itself whether he enjoyed politics (no), whether he knew what he was doing (no) and whether he was qualified (also no).

The signs went viral far beyond the Okanagan. Kelowna's municipal election on October 17 had 37 candidates chasing eight council seats, so standing out was half the battle.

The count

When the preliminary results came in, Rogers had 12,635 votes. That put him fourth out of 37, comfortably inside the winning eight.

The person who missed out was Maxine DeHart, a councillor first elected in 2011 who was going for a fifth term. She finished ninth with 11,165 votes, just 305 short of the last seat. Five newcomers and three incumbents made it onto the new council, on a turnout of about 31 percent.

So was the whole thing a joke? Only partly. Rogers explained that the self-mockery was deliberate and that he does have useful experience in business and problem-solving.

"I'm being a little bit tongue-in-cheek when I say I'm unqualified," Rogers told Global News.

He says his priorities are homelessness, housing costs and support for small businesses, and after the results came in he promised to take the job seriously. The man who barely wanted to do it now has a four-year term to prove how hard it can be.
