An Ohio Town Spends 45 Days Failing to Catch One Runaway Sheep. Then a Teenager Rides In on Horseback

There was a Facebook event, a police department, an animal sanctuary and a lot of volunteers. None of it worked until the rope came out.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 12, 2026 · Wadsworth, Ohio, USA · 3 min read

In September, people in Wadsworth, Ohio, started reporting a sheep wandering around town. Nobody knew whose it was, and nobody could get near it.

She was an American blackbelly, a hardy, athletic breed that turned out to be very good at not being caught. Over the following weeks she turned up in yards and fields, then vanished again.

The hunt grew into a town project. Janine Smalley, who runs Whispering Acres Farm Animal Sanctuary, and volunteer Rebecca Roxberry led the effort, a Facebook event called Catching the Wadsworth Sheep gathered sightings, and the Wadsworth Police Department joined in posting updates.

Enter the cowgirl

She was finally spotted in a field off U.S. Route 224, near a KFC, and guided back to the spot where Roxberry had been leaving food for her. That is where teenager Reagan Crumpler arrived on her 18-year-old horse, Catalena, with a rope.

Even then it was not quick. Crumpler said it took hours of chasing and several near misses before her lasso finally landed. Roxberry announced the capture that afternoon, ending a 45-day saga.

"All that mattered is that we caught her, she's safe and sound, and she's in the best of care," Roxberry told News 5 Cleveland.

The sheep's new name includes Reagan, in honour of the girl who caught her. She is a little thin after six weeks on the run, so she is on a refeeding plan and due a vet check at the sanctuary.

Her owner has still not come forward. If nobody does, the escape artist has a permanent home at Whispering Acres.
