Student Misses a $1 Million Kick on Live TV. Then a Hollywood Star Steps In

He camped out all night for one shot at a fortune, kicked barefoot, and missed. Then a famous voice in the crowd changed the rules.

By RetardedNews Staff · September 12, 2026 · Austin, Texas, USA · 3 min read

ESPN's College GameDay show has a segment called Kicking Is Easy. A fan picked from the crowd gets one field goal attempt, and if it goes through, host Pat McAfee pays out a huge prize. It is usually a sad moment for someone.

Before Texas played Ohio State on September 12, the show was in Austin. Connor Kacena-Merrell, a Texas freshman who grew up near Indianapolis, camped out the night before so he would be among the first 300 fans eligible for the draw. His name came up.

The prize was $1 million. Before he kicked, he announced that he would give half of anything he won to Building Tomorrow, an Indianapolis charity that funds schooling in East Africa. Then he took off his shoes and kicked barefoot.

The miss

The ball missed. Normally that is the end of it. But the actor Matthew McConaughey, a famous Longhorns fan, was on the set, and he talked McAfee into giving the student another go. McConaughey added $100,000 to the pot himself. Analyst Kirk Herbstreit matched it.

So the second attempt was worth $1.2 million, the biggest prize in the show's history. Kacena-Merrell put it through.

"The best time to plant a tree was yesterday. The second best time is right now," he said, explaining the donation, according to Nexstar.

The day was not over. The charity arm of Tito's Vodka offered another $1 million to good causes if he could make a 35-yard kick during a break in the first quarter of the game. He made that one on his first try. Half went to the Central Texas Food Bank and half to Building Tomorrow.

Add it all up and the morning he almost went home with nothing raised around $2.2 million, most of it for charity. He says his own share will go mostly on out-of-state tuition, with a summer trip with friends on top.
