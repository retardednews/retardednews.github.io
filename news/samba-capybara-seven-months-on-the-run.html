A Zoo Animal Escaped Less Than a Day After Arriving. Seven Months Later, Staff Know Where She Is and Still Can't Bring Her Home

Drones, a sniffer dog and 150 reported sightings later, she keeps turning up on camera. Even some of the cameras have gone missing.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 28, 2026 · Winchester, Hampshire, England · 3 min read

In March, two young capybaras named Samba and Tango arrived at Marwell Zoo near Winchester, in southern England. Less than 24 hours later, on March 17, they had broken out of their temporary enclosure.

Tango was found nearby almost straight away. Samba, described by staff as the more adventurous sister, was not.

Capybaras are the world's largest rodents, native to South America and very much at home in water. Samba was soon spotted in the nearby village of Owslebury and later along the River Itchen, where people reported seeing her sunbathing on the bank and swimming downstream.

The search that never ends

The zoo threw everything at it: daily ground searches, thermal drones over the villages, a sniffer dog and trail cameras. At one point two of those cameras were stolen, which made the job even harder.

In July the zoo brought in a male capybara, Ripple, to keep Tango company, while insisting he was no replacement for Samba. In August cameras caught her again, and more were installed.

In October came new pictures. They are blurry, but they prove Samba is still out there. The zoo won't say exactly where, because the photos were taken on private land.

The frustrating part is that staff now have a good idea where she is. They say they can't simply walk up and grab her, because frightening her could send her off somewhere new. Instead they have set up a feeding station to get her used to visiting one spot, and describe her return as a slow, carefully managed process.

Seven months in, England's most famous runaway rodent is still winning.
