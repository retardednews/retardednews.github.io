Man Hollows Out a 1,214-Pound Pumpkin and Paddles It 46 Miles Down a River for a World Record

He named it Punky Loafster. It nearly sank. He paddled through the night anyway.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 26, 2024 · Columbia River, Washington and Oregon, USA · 3 min read

Cinderella turned a pumpkin into a carriage. One Oregon man turned his into a boat and set off down one of America's biggest rivers.

The 46-year-old giant-pumpkin grower from Happy Valley, Oregon, raised a 1,214-pound monster he named Punky Loafster. He pollinated it in July, harvested it in early October, and a week later carved it into a vessel.

On October 12 and 13, 2024, he paddled it 73.5 kilometres, about 46 miles, down the Columbia River from North Bonneville to Vancouver, Washington. The trip took roughly 26 hours over three legs and smashed the previous Guinness record of 63 kilometres.

Rough seas, soggy squash

He wrote the words it's real on both sides of his pumpkin so people watching from the shore would not think it was a prop. Winds of around 35 mph sent waves crashing over the sides, and the pumpkin began taking on water.

At a stop near Multnomah Falls, he lightened his boat by scooping out more pumpkin flesh with a soup can. He then paddled through the night, because pulling up on a rocky beach risked puncturing the hull.

Kayakers and a pontoon boat followed him the whole way, just in case the pumpkin gave up before he did.

He has been growing giant pumpkins since 2011 and racing them in a local pumpkin regatta since 2013, which raises the question of how many other people are out there quietly training for this.
