Two Knights in Full Armour Charge at Each Other in a London Park. Their Horses Are Rental E-Bikes

Hundreds turned up, millions watched online, and the bike company had to issue a statement. Then a real professional jouster gave his verdict.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 18, 2026 · Hackney, London, England · 3 min read

On a Saturday in mid-October, hundreds of people gathered in London Fields, a park in Hackney, east London, for a free event that sounded like a joke. Two men in full suits of armour would fight a medieval joust, lances and all.

The catch was the horses. There weren't any. Artist and online personality Jack Carden and his opponent, who performs as Camtholomew the First, rode the green Lime electric bikes that Londoners rent by the minute to get to work.

Clips of the two knights wobbling toward each other with lances under their arms spread fast online and were viewed millions of times. Carden called the whole thing performance art.

The company is not amused

Lime, which owns the bikes, was asked what it thought of its fleet being used as warhorses. Its answer, given to the BBC, was polite but clear: the bikes are for getting around the city, not for medieval combat.

"While we appreciate the medieval spirit, safety must always come first," a Lime spokesperson told the BBC.

That did not stop the sequel. The organisers announced Lime Bike Jousting 2, subtitled No Horses, No Mercy, and LBC reported that more than 5,000 people RSVPed within a day. Police signed off on the gathering about 90 minutes before it started on Halloween, and only then was the location revealed: London Fields again.

Then the experts weighed in. ITV asked Andy Deane, captain of the jousting team at the Royal Armouries in Leeds, who does this for a living on real horses. He had no complaints. He called the e-bike version absolutely a legitimate form of jousting.

So London now has an unofficial new sport. The armour is real, the lances are real, and the steeds have a pay-per-minute app.
