Two Men Took the Maker of Reese's to Court Over Their Halloween Candy. A Judge Has Now Said No Twice

Nobody said it tasted bad. The complaint was about something the chocolate didn't have.

By RetardedNews Staff · September 17, 2026 · Fort Lauderdale, Florida, USA · 3 min read

Every autumn, Reese's Peanut Butter Pumpkins arrive in shops wrapped in orange packaging printed with a cheerful carved jack-o'-lantern: triangle eyes, a nose and a toothy grin.

Unwrap one, though, and the chocolate inside is a smooth, plain, entirely faceless lump.

Two Florida men, Nathan Vidal and Eduardo Granados, decided that amounted to false advertising. In 2024 they filed a proposed class action against Hershey, saying they had bought the candy for its novelty carvings and paid extra for a face that never turned up.

The judge isn't carving

In September 2025, U.S. District Judge Melissa Damian threw out the first version of the complaint but let them try again. They did. In September 2026 she dismissed it a second time.

The judge found the men had received edible Reese's products "of the type and quantity advertised," and that what they described was "subjective disappointment" rather than a real financial loss.

Their claim that they had paid a premium ran into trouble too. They bought the candy at Publix but used prices from Walmart, Target and Hershey's own online store to make their case. And the carved face on the wrapper is labelled, in small print, a decorating suggestion.

The pumpkins are not the only alleged offenders. Hershey has faced similar complaints about Reese's ghosts with no faces and Reese's footballs with no laces.

The men's lawyer told Reuters they plan to appeal, saying Florida consumers deserve to get what they pay for. Hershey said it does not comment on litigation.
