Man Tries On a $292,000 Watch and Sprints Off Still Wearing It. Three Days Later, a Parcel Arrives

Inside was a very short handwritten message. Police still don't know who wrote it.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 2, 2026 · Nakano, Tokyo, Japan · 3 min read

On the evening of Thursday, October 29, a man walked into Kamekichi, a luxury watch shop on the third floor of Nakano Broadway, a maze-like Tokyo shopping centre famous for its collectors' stores.

He spent about eight minutes looking around and asked to try on an Audemars Piguet valued at about 46 million yen, roughly $292,000. Then, with the watch still on his left wrist, he ran.

Staff chased him shouting thief as he leapt from escalator to escalator, all the way down from the third floor to the basement and out of the building. Security cameras caught him running down the street. Nobody was hurt.

Return to sender

On Sunday morning, November 1, a courier dropped a cardboard box at the shop. Inside was the watch, with no obvious damage. Employees checked the serial number. It was the one.

Tucked in with it was a handwritten note. According to Japanese television, it said simply: I'm sorry.

The parcel had been sent from Aichi Prefecture, hundreds of kilometres away, and carried a sender's name and address. Investigators believe both are fake.

Police are now trying to identify whoever posted the box, and whether it was the same man who fled the shop. As of early November, nobody had been arrested.

It is not the building's first watch theft this year. In August, four watches worth a combined 200 million yen were taken from a ground-floor shop in the same complex, and two men were arrested and charged.
