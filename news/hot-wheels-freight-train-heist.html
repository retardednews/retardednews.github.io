At 3 a.m. a Caller Tells Police: 'They're Robbing the Trains.' The Loot Turns Out to Be Tiny

Officers stopped a pickup loaded with 350 boxes. The cargo inside was worth about $54,000, and it all fits in your hand.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 23, 2026 · Colton, California, USA · 3 min read

In the small hours of October 20, a resident of Colton, an old railway town east of Los Angeles, rang the police with a tip that sounded like it came straight out of a Western.

"Yeah, they're robbing the trains down here at the end of Maple Street," the caller told dispatchers, according to the Los Angeles Times.

A BNSF freight train was sitting near Mason Street, and the caller said people were loading boxes from it into a black pickup truck.

One officer happened to be parked under a nearby bridge. He spotted a black Chevrolet Silverado, followed it and pulled it over.

What was in the boxes

The truck was crammed with Hot Wheels. Officers counted 350 boxes of the little die-cast cars, each holding several models, with an estimated total value of around $54,000.

The driver, a 20-year-old man, was booked on suspicion of burglary, possession of stolen property and grand theft, and was later released on bail. Detectives are still trying to work out whether he had help.

The Colton Police Department could not resist the obvious joke. Its Facebook post about the arrest announced that his wheels just weren't hot enough to get away.

A department spokesperson told reporters an arrest of that size was a big deal for the city. Freight-train break-ins are a long-running headache along Southern California's rail lines, but few of them come with a pun this good.
