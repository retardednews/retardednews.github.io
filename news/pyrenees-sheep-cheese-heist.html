Thieves Break Into a French Farm, Knock Out the Camera and Take €25,000 of Goods. They Were Very Picky

They passed over the younger stock. The farmer is convinced they knew exactly what they were looking for.

By RetardedNews Staff · September 25, 2026 · Artigueloutan, Pyrénées-Atlantiques, France · 3 min read

At Bergerie du Tilh, a sheep farm in Artigueloutan near Pau in south-west France, the most valuable room on the property is not the farmhouse. It is the cool ageing room where the cheese sits for months until it is ready.

Two people forced their way into that room, put a security camera out of action and left with about 250 wheels of sheep's-milk tomme.

Connoisseur criminals

They did not just grab whatever was closest to the door. According to French broadcaster TF1, the thieves went for the older, darker wheels, the ones that had matured longest and sell for the most. Each wheel goes for between €80 and €130 depending on its age.

Producer Régis Junqua put the loss at roughly €25,000. He told TF1 the intruders seemed calm and well prepared, and described them as professionals.

Getting rid of 250 marked cheeses is harder than it sounds, because every wheel carries the producer's stamp. Alexandre Salles, co-president of a French cheesemakers' federation, suggested a buyer could simply strip off the rind, cut the cheese into pieces and sell it where nobody would recognise it.

It is not an isolated taste for dairy. In late 2025, Italian police investigated the disappearance of around 200 wheels of Parmigiano Reggiano from a cooperative near Bologna.

A France 24 press review in November relayed a report in The Times that cheese has become France's most stolen food, with thieves said to like it partly because the penalties are far lighter than for dealing drugs. No arrests had been reported in the Pyrenees case.
