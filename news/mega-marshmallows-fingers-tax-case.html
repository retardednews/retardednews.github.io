Seven Years of Court Battles Over One Question: Do You Eat a Giant Marshmallow With Your Fingers?

Nearly half a million pounds rode on the answer, and the UK tax office would not let it go.

By RetardedNews Staff · April 3, 2026 · London, England, UK · 3 min read

In British tax law, most food is free of VAT, but sweets are taxed at 20 percent. The legal definition of a sweet includes a phrase that turned out to be worth a small fortune: sweetened prepared food that is normally eaten with the fingers.

Innovative Bites, a London wholesaler of American candy, sells Mega Marshmallows, which are far bigger than the usual British supermarket kind. It treated them as food. In 2019 HMRC disagreed and sent a bill for £472,928 covering four years of sales.

Skewer versus fingers

The company's case was that these giants are not really a snack. They are marketed for roasting over a campfire or barbecue, shops stock them next to the barbecue gear, they sell best in summer and they end up in s'mores. In other words, they go on a stick.

It won at the First-tier Tribunal in 2022 and again at the Upper Tribunal in 2024. HMRC kept going. In March 2025 the Court of Appeal said the real test was simply whether the marshmallows are normally eaten with the fingers, and sent the case back to a fresh tribunal to decide exactly that.

That tribunal has now ruled. It found Mega Marshmallows are more often eaten without fingers than with them, and that a roasted one eaten straight off the skewer does not count as finger food. The £472,928 bill was cancelled in full.

Robin Prince of accountancy firm MHA called the UK's VAT rules on food "absurd" and urged the government to overhaul them.

Commentators pointed out the odd result. Mini marshmallows sold for baking are tax-free, standard ones are taxed as sweets, and the giant ones are tax-free again.

After seven years and four rounds in court, the official answer is in: you eat these with a stick.
