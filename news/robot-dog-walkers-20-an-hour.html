San Francisco Startup Pays $20 an Hour to Walk Its Dogs. Read the Small Print Before You Apply

No experience needed, paid training and cash after every shift. You just have to be able to lift 33 pounds, for one very specific reason.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 27, 2026 · San Francisco, California, USA · 3 min read

In early October a job ad appeared in a Bay Area jobs group on Facebook that looked like an easy gig: walk dogs around San Francisco for four to six hours at a time, $20 an hour, paid as soon as each shift ends. No experience necessary.

The catch was in the details. The dogs are robots, about $2,000 apiece from Chinese maker Unitree, and the employer is Dimensional, an AI startup in the city's Dogpatch neighbourhood that has bought more than 100 of them, plus around 50 humanoid robots.

The walkers steer the machines along outdoor routes with a remote or a phone app while the robots' cameras and sensors record everything. That data is used to teach robots how to find their way through real city streets.

Why you need strong arms

Applicants had to be comfortable handling about 33 pounds of equipment. That is roughly what one of the robot dogs weighs, and there is a reason it matters: sometimes the dogs overheat or simply stop working in the middle of a walk, and then the walker has to carry the dog back to the office.

The company is building open-source software that would let AI agents control robots doing manual jobs like hauling boxes or working on building sites. Its 26-year-old founder, Stash Pomichter, told The New York Times that humans still doing that kind of heavy physical work is 'honestly barbaric'.

But the walks taught him something humbling about the machines. "What we determined from our dog-walking experiment was that agents are still very bad at controlling hardware," Pomichter said.

In other words, the robots still need a person holding the leash, and now and then carrying the dog. The original Facebook post has since been deleted.
