Spain Orders €258 Million of New Trains, Then Finds Out They Won't Fit Through the Tunnels

Someone got the measurements wrong. Two of the country's top transport officials lost their jobs.

By RetardedNews Staff · February 21, 2023 · Asturias and Cantabria, Spain · 3 min read

Measure twice, cut once. It is advice every carpenter knows, and apparently advice nobody passed on to Spain's railways.

In June 2020, Renfe, Spain's state rail operator, ordered 31 new commuter trains worth about €258 million from the Spanish manufacturer CAF. They were meant for the narrow-gauge lines that wind through the mountains of Asturias and Cantabria in the north of the country.

There was just one problem. The measurements CAF had been given were wrong. In March 2021, the manufacturer realised the trains would be too wide to pass through some of the tunnels on the route, and stopped building.

Blame the 19th century

The tunnels in question were dug in the 1800s, and their dimensions do not match modern standards. Somewhere between the old tunnels and the new trains, nobody checked.

When the story finally became public in February 2023, the fallout was swift. The president of Renfe and Spain's secretary of state for transport both resigned, after two senior rail officials had already been fired.

Cantabria's regional president called it a monumental botch, demanded that heads roll, and asked for compensation.

The government and Renfe insisted no money had been wasted because the error was caught before any trains were built. Delivery of the new trains slipped from 2024 to 2026, and rail officials launched an inquiry into how a nine-figure order ended up depending on the wrong tape measure.
