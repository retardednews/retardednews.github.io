Swiss Scientists Teach a Robot Hand to Do Something No Hand Should Ever Do

It weighs less than a bag of sugar, carries its own battery, and when it falls over, it gets straight back up.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 30, 2026 · Zurich, Switzerland · 3 min read

Robot hands are usually attached to robot arms. The team at ETH Zurich's Soft Robotics Lab decided theirs did not need one.

Starting with an off-the-shelf robotic hand, the researchers strapped a battery, a tiny computer and a motion sensor onto its back. The whole thing weighs 818 grams, and nothing connects it to anything else.

Then they flipped it over and taught it to walk on its fingertips, scuttling across the floor just like Thing, the disembodied hand from The Addams Family. Almost every outlet that covered the research made the same comparison, and the videos are about as unsettling as you would expect.

How do you teach a hand to walk?

The hand learned in a computer simulation first, through trial and error, and the skills were then moved onto the real hardware. It managed to crawl over 14 different surfaces, including wooden floors, metal grating, grass and gravel.

Lead author Amirhossein Kazemipour explained the trickiest part: "The fingers have different lengths and a thumb off to one side, which makes walking awkward."

It can also right itself after toppling over, which it did in 21 out of 25 tries, and balance on some fingers while pressing keys with another. In one test it hit the correct arrow key 29 times out of 32, and it even played moves in the puzzle game Sokoban, with a human choosing the moves.

The team's plan is for a bigger robot to drop the hand near a tight space, let it crawl in to press a button or move something, and then come back. Detaching and reattaching on its own is still a future goal, and the study is a preprint that has not yet been peer-reviewed. So for now, at least, the hand does not wander off by itself.
