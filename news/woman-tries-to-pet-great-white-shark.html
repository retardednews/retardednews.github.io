Woman Reaches Over the Side of a Boat to Pet a Great White Shark. The Shark Has Other Ideas

A tagging trip off Georgia produced a viral clip of an apex predator swinging round at a hand that should not have been there.

By RetardedNews Staff · October 28, 2026 · Tybee Island, Georgia, USA · 3 min read

There are animals you pet, and there are animals you photograph from a respectful distance. The great white shark is firmly in the second group.

Off Tybee Island, Georgia, a guest on a research trip with the Knot Lucky White Shark Tagging Program reached over the side of the boat toward a great white swimming alongside.

The shark thrashed, swung round and lunged toward her. She pulled her hand back just in time, and she was not hurt.

The crew says it was under control

The guest, a military veteran named Jen, was on the trip as part of the program. Founder Jimmy Armel said she was never in real danger, because the crew had the shark under control the whole time.

Not everyone was reassured. University of Sydney shark researcher Chris Pepin-Neff criticised the attempt to touch the animal.

The shark was not tagged. The lesson, hopefully, was.

The shark itself could not be tagged because of safety concerns for the crew and the animal. It swam off having made its position on petting very clear.
