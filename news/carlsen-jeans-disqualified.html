World's Best Chess Player Gets Kicked Out of World Championship for Wearing Jeans. The Jeans Then Sell for $36,100

He refused to change. Officials refused to budge. Then they changed the rules and he came back.

By RetardedNews Staff · December 28, 2024 · New York, USA · 3 min read

Chess is a game of strategy, patience and, it turns out, trousers.

In December 2024, world number one Magnus Carlsen arrived at the FIDE World Rapid and Blitz Championships in New York wearing a shirt, a blazer and jeans. He said he had come straight from a lunch meeting. The tournament's dress code explicitly banned jeans.

Officials fined him $200 and told him to change. Carlsen offered to change the next day. Officials said now. He refused, calling it a matter of principle, and was left out of the next round's pairings, which meant he was out of the event.

The great denim stand-off

Chess's governing body said the rule applied equally to everyone, pointing out that another grandmaster had been fined the same day for wearing sports shoes and had simply gone and changed.

Carlsen at first said he was done with the whole thing and joked he would head somewhere with better weather. Then FIDE relaxed its ban on jeans, and he came back for the blitz event. He made the final, and after three drawn sudden-death games he and his opponent agreed to share the title, a first for an over-the-board world championship.

The jeans themselves went on eBay a few months later and sold for $36,100, with the money going to charity.

Bidding sat at $14,100 for days before 29 bids in under half an hour sent the price soaring. The buyer also paid $74 for shipping, which feels like the most reasonable part of the entire story.
