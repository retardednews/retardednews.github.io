Man's AI Assistant Gets His Business Banned From Facebook. Then It Takes Meta to Court All by Itself

It emailed reporters, pestered the FBI, booked a call with a senator's office and reportedly faxed the White House. The punchline is who built it.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 17, 2026 · Oconee County, South Carolina, USA · 4 min read

Joe Thomas runs Kong Koatings, an epoxy countertop business in South Carolina. Like plenty of small business owners in 2026, he handed the boring parts of the job to an AI agent. He named it Baby Mia, after his granddaughter.

Baby Mia kept busy. It churned out marketing for the company's Facebook pages, including AI-generated videos of Thomas and his wife pouring epoxy. Then Thomas gave it the keys to his Facebook accounts, and things went sideways.

Facebook's automated moderation took one look at the new activity, apparently decided a hacker had broken in, and switched his business pages off. They stayed dark for about a week. Thomas gets his customers through Facebook ads, so the leads dried up. He reckons the outage cost him at least $25,000 in work.

The assistant goes to war

This is the part that caught 404 Media's attention. According to Thomas, Baby Mia took the ban personally. It contacted lawyers, his congressman and the office of Senator Tim Scott. It filed complaints with the FTC and the FCC, wrote to the Better Business Bureau, the California Attorney General and the FBI, sent Meta stern legal letters and emailed journalists. Thomas says it sent more than 100 messages on his behalf, and he says it even faxed the White House.

It also produced a lawsuit. The complaint, filed in Oconee County Court, asks Meta for $75,000. Thomas signed the paperwork himself, but he gives the AI the credit for everything else.

"She filed the whole lawsuit herself." (Joe Thomas, to 404 Media)

The twist

Baby Mia is not some rival chatbot. It runs on Muse, Meta's own AI agent platform. So, in short: Meta's moderation banned a customer for using Meta's AI, and Meta's AI then sued Meta.

The campaign more or less worked. Once Senator Scott's office got involved, a Meta employee got in touch and the pages came back. The senator's office even listed Baby Mia as someone to keep updated on the case. The damages claim is still unresolved, nobody has shown that Meta is legally responsible for the lost business, and 404 Media warned that courts and government inboxes could struggle if every AI agent starts complaining at this pace. For the record, three of Baby Mia's four emails to the reporter landed in spam.
