Hundreds Show Up to Push Chicago's 110-Ton Bean Exactly Seven Inches to the Left. Their Reason Is Wonderful

Anonymous flyers, a start time of precisely 12:47 p.m. and a very specific rescue mission. Then a rival city decided it could do better.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 8, 2026 · Millennium Park, Chicago, Illinois, USA · 3 min read

In early November, flyers started appearing around Chicago and online. They made one demand and offered no explanation: the Bean needed to move seven inches to the left.

The Bean, officially Cloud Gate, is Anish Kapoor's giant mirrored sculpture in Millennium Park. It has stood in the same spot since 2004 and weighs about 110 tons. Nobody let that put them off.

On Sunday, November 8, at exactly 12:47 p.m., the crowd arrived. One art student who joined in estimated that about 250 people turned up to press their shoulders against the polished steel and shove.

So why seven inches?

The event's invitation page explained the mission. It claimed a man is trapped inside the Bean, and that new "research" suggested a latch door under the sculpture's right side. Slide the whole thing seven inches over, the theory went, and people could finally reach him.

The gag built on earlier joke protests at the sculpture and a mockumentary that had recently appeared on YouTube. Nobody expected to rescue anyone, or to shift 110 tons of steel by hand. The student said the real point was bringing people together.

Chicago entertainer Taylor Bennett, who helped rally the crowd, first claimed a partial win because someone said it had moved an inch. He later admitted the Bean had not moved at all.

St. Louis wants a turn

A week later the joke crossed state lines. On November 15, at 12:07 p.m., a group in St. Louis gathered to push the Gateway Arch seven inches to the left. A local Instagram account had remixed the Chicago flyer, and its post drew more than 21,000 reactions.

"I just feel like we're stronger than Chicago." (Megan Estopare of STL Ain't Bad, to St. Louis Public Radio)

The St. Louis push opened with a prayer and a countdown, and one man brought a tape measure and announced success. The Arch weighs more than 17,000 tons and its foundations go 30 feet into bedrock, so it stayed put too. The man inside the Bean is presumably still waiting.
