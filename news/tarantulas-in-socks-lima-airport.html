Airport Staff Notice Socks Are Moving. Inside: More Than 3,000 Tarantulas

Three travellers tried to fly from Lima to Europe with a suitcase zoo of eggs, babies and adult spiders stuffed into dirty socks.

By RetardedNews Staff · November 4, 2026 · Lima, Peru · 3 min read

Most people pack socks to wear them. Three travellers at Lima's Jorge Chávez International Airport packed socks to hide tarantulas in them. More than 3,000 of them.

Acting on an anonymous tip, officers stopped the group, two German citizens and one Polish citizen, as they were checking in for a flight to Europe in early November 2026.

Inside three suitcases were dozens of socks, and inside the socks were tarantulas at every stage of life: eggs, juveniles and fully grown adults.

A very expensive laundry bag

Peru's National Forest and Wildlife Service, SERFOR, said the spiders were bound for Germany, where exotic tarantulas are prized by collectors.

One outlet estimated a single tarantula can fetch around 500 euros in Europe, which would put the sock collection at roughly 1.5 million euros. That figure has not been officially confirmed.

Somewhere in Germany, a collector is still waiting for a very hairy delivery.

The three were handed to prosecutors. In Peru, wildlife trafficking can mean three to five years in prison, rising to seven when protected species are involved. Socks are a recurring theme: Lima officers caught a man with hundreds of tarantulas strapped to his body in 2024.
