Beavers Finish Government's 7-Year Dam Project Overnight, Save Taxpayers $1.2 Million

Officials spent years on permits and land talks. A family of rodents spent a couple of nights on it and did a better job.

By RetardedNews Staff · February 11, 2025 · Brdy Protected Landscape Area, Czech Republic · 3 min read

Every government has a project that seems to take forever. In the Czech Republic, one of them was finally finished, and not by anyone on the payroll.

Conservation officials had wanted to build barriers near the Klabava River, on a former army training ground south-west of Prague that became a protected area in 2016. The goal was to shield the river, and its critically endangered crayfish, from sediment and acidic water draining out of two nearby ponds, and to create a wetland along the way.

The plan was drawn up in 2018. It was approved. It had a budget of about 30 million koruna, roughly $1.2 million. And then it sat there for years, stuck in negotiations over land and permits between military forest managers and the river basin authority.

Enter the beavers

Before a single excavator arrived, a local beaver family of around eight animals took matters into their own paws. They built at least four dams across a drainage gully soldiers had once dug, with more under construction.

The resulting network of pools and channels covers about twice the area the human planners had in mind. The head of the protected-area administration said the beavers had created the habitat almost overnight, with no paperwork and at no cost, saving the state the entire 30 million crowns.

A zoologist pointed out the key difference: beavers can build a dam in a night or two, while people need permits and funding first.

Officials said they do not expect any conflict with their new unpaid contractors for at least a decade. The nearest farmland that could be affected is far from the site. The beavers have not submitted an invoice.
