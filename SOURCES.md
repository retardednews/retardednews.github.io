# Story sources

Places to find weird but real news. Always trace a story back to the original report (local paper, police statement, court record, study) before writing it up. Aggregators often get details wrong.

## Picking topics: clickbait first
When scanning these sources, take only the most clickbaity and funny stories. A story has to be both. If it's merely odd, or funny but has no hook, skip it, and when in doubt, leave it out. The same goes for site headlines. See [FACEBOOK.md](FACEBOOK.md) for the posting side.
- **Strong hook:** you can tease it in one line that makes people need to know more.
- **Hidden payoff:** there's a twist, reveal or punchline you can hold back until the reader clicks.
- **Picture that raises a question:** a strange photo beats a plain one.
- **Skip** stories whose whole point fits in the headline, since nobody needs to click.
- **Headlines on the site:** tease the story, don't spoil it, and keep it true to the article (the verification checklist still applies).

## Writing rules: own words, no copyrighted material
- Rewrite every story completely in our own words. Never copy or lightly reword a source's sentences.
- Combine facts from several sources (at least 2 where possible) into one fresh write-up, and list them all under sources.
- Quotes: only short ones the sources actually contain, attributed, and at most one or two per story. Paraphrase everything else.
- Images: only free-licensed Wikimedia Commons files with author and license credited. Never use photos from news sites, social media or agencies, even with credit.
- Headlines and decks are ours too. Don't reuse the source's headline.

## Weird-news specialists
- [Oddity Central](https://www.odditycentral.com/): odd people, records, bizarre hobbies, with an Asia focus. Good pictures.
- [LADbible](https://www.ladbible.com/): viral, lifestyle and "you won't believe" stories. Sourcing is often social media, so verify.
- [UPI Odd News](https://www.upi.com/Odd_News/): short, factual, several items a day. Great for records, animals and lucky finds.
- [Reuters Oddly Enough](https://www.reuters.com/lifestyle/oddly-enough/): wire-service reliable.
- [AP Beyond the Headlines](https://apnews.com/beyond-the-headlines): AP's oddities and human-interest feed. Wire-service reliable, mostly US.
- [Sky News Offbeat](https://news.sky.com/strange-news): UK and global quirky stories.
- [Metro: Weird](https://metro.co.uk/news/weird/): high volume, tabloid tone.
- [Newsweek: Viral/Trending](https://www.newsweek.com/): pet and Reddit/TikTok-driven stories.
- [Fark](https://www.fark.com/): link aggregator. Mines every outlet for absurd headlines.
- [r/nottheonion](https://www.reddit.com/r/nottheonion/): real headlines that sound like satire. Each one links its source.
- [Unilad](https://www.unilad.com/): same style as LADbible.
- [The Daily Star (UK)](https://www.dailystar.co.uk/news/weird-news/): very tabloid, so verify everything.
- [Mothership.sg](https://mothership.sg/): odd Singapore and Southeast Asia stories.
- [SoraNews24](https://soranews24.com/): weird Japan.
- [Odd Stuff Magazine](https://www.oddstuffmagazine.com/): curiosities and records.

## Categories that keep paying off
- Animals: [The Dodo](https://www.thedodo.com/), [Live Science: Animals](https://www.livescience.com/animals)
- Science oddities: [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/), [IFLScience](https://www.iflscience.com/), [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/)
- Crime and courts: local police Facebook pages, [Florida Man trackers](https://www.reddit.com/r/FloridaMan/), [Law & Crime](https://lawandcrime.com/)
- Records: [Guinness World Records news](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news)
- Tech fails: [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/), [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/)
- Regional quirky news: [BBC News: Stories](https://www.bbc.com/news/stories), [The Guardian: Weird news](https://www.theguardian.com/world/weird-news), [Japan Today: Offbeat](https://japantoday.com/category/offbeat), [Central European News](https://www.cen.news/)

## Verification checklist
1. Find the original source (local outlet, police or court document, study).
2. Confirm the date. Old stories get recycled as new.
3. Check the photo with a reverse image search (Google Lens or TinEye).
4. Be extra careful with anything that only appears on tabloids or LADbible-style sites.
